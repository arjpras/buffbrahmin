My Story

Built by a Desi, for Desis.

I'm Arjun, founder of DesiFit Method. I built this because the fitness advice we grew up with was either "just eat less rice" or "go run." Neither one worked. Neither one respected our food, our families, or our reality.

The Origin

How DesiFit Method started.

Growing up South Asian, I saw and felt the friction of trying to balance cultural norms with fitness goals. Whether that was staying in shape for sport, building muscle, or losing weight. For me, it was building muscle. I was vegetarian most of my life and stopped because I thought I had to in order to make any real progress.

I quickly realized that wasn't true.

The advice given to us was usually extreme: cut out rice, cut out roti, cut out dosa, cut out ghee, run for hours, follow plans that weren't built for real life. Many of my friends on the same journey either burned out or gave up altogether. The few who didn't give up were left feeling alienated from the food, family meals, and traditions they grew up loving.

So I developed a different approach.

I started focusing on simple, intense training programs. Sustainable nutrition that optimized protein and key nutrients without obsessing over them. And long-term health, including gut health, which most coaches ignore entirely. That let me make changes I could actually stick to. It let me enjoy outings with friends and family without sacrificing the food and lifestyle I grew up on.

Whether you're balancing work, family, school, or all three at once, there's a way to prioritize fitness the Desi way. That's what this program is built to deliver.

What I Believe

Principles behind every program.

Consistency > intensity

Showing up for 30 minutes every day beats 3-hour gym sessions you can't sustain. Real growth happens in the boring middle. The simple training and habits you actually keep doing.

Simple > extreme

You don't need to cut all carbs, run for hours, or train every day. Small, sustainable changes repeated daily will outperform every fad I've ever seen, including the ones I tried first.

Culture > conformity

You should never have to choose between health and the foods you grew up loving. Dal, rice, dosa, idli, sambar, ghee, paneer, chai. They all stay. We just upgrade the supporting cast.

Coaching philosophy

"Believe it or not, fitness is mostly about diet. Much less about hours in the gym."

The simple habits that keep you in shape come from incredibly small fixes in the kitchen. The most important thing is sticking to your lifestyle changes, not bouncing back from extreme ones.
